**Completed changes**

| Area | Problem found | Deployed result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Publishing | A successful upload response could trigger cleanup before the requested privacy or schedule was checked. Interrupted transfers and repeated clicks could leave uncertain outcomes. | Saves the resumable session before sending video bytes and saves the returned video ID before readback. Confirms upload state, privacy and the exact publishing timestamp before deleting media. An existing session or video ID blocks a fresh duplicate upload. |
| Review | Fact, scene and final-package review were not consistently enforced; later edits could leave an old approval usable. | Requires an independent source link and notes, exact-scene confirmation, and full render/package review. Render and approval fingerprints invalidate approval after relevant edits or file changes. These record human verification; they do not independently prove a claim. |
| Daily batches | Paused or rejected work could still reach later production actions. Failed jobs could lose their reserved slot. | Enforces one explicitly started day with three slots at 13:00, 15:00 and 22:00 WAT. Paused/rejected jobs cannot continue production. Failed jobs retain their slot; rejection releases it. The batch pauses after three confirmed uploads and never starts another day automatically. |
| Duplicate prevention | Checks could miss work already in the queue or accept malformed/uncertain model results. Direct URLs needed the same checks. | Screens against channel history and pending jobs, validates actual YouTube URLs, applies screening to direct sources, and routes malformed or uncertain comparisons to review. Refreshed history contains 44 uploads; existing leads require fresh screening. |
| Cloudflare requests | Large media requests and long renders/uploads could exceed proxy limits or time out while the server continued working. | Transfers media in 8 MiB chunks with offset checks, progress and retry handling. Long UI actions return a task ID immediately and poll for the result. A service restart tells the operator to inspect the existing job before retrying. |
| Rendering | Synchronous encoding could block the dashboard; missing audio and overflowing hook text needed explicit failures. | Uses bounded asynchronous FFmpeg work and a lighter review preview. Rejects missing scene audio and excessive hook text. Keeps the approved charcoal card, real avatar, rounded movie window, silent gameplay, scene-only audio and 1080×1920/30 fps format. |
| UI/UX | Mobile review, status clarity, form refreshes and action availability needed improvement. | Added clear day/start/pause controls, accurate filled-slot states, source search, archived completed/rejected work, a responsive portrait review dialog, explicit verification steps, persistent errors and upload progress. Forms keep edits during polling; buttons recover after operations. Added labeled controls, keyboard access, larger touch targets and reduced-motion support. |
| Web security | State-changing routes and OAuth state needed stronger request binding. | Added same-origin/CSRF checks, signed OAuth state with expiry and channel identity validation, strict script CSP, security headers and protected atomic credential writes. Credential values, raw model responses and resumable-session URLs are excluded from dashboard state. |
| Hosting and recovery | No verified project backup routine was found in the bounded inventory. Rendering needed resource limits on the shared VPS. | Enabled a dedicated Cloudflare service and daily private SQLite/credential backups. Verified a separate restored database copy. Retains seven snapshots. App limits are two CPU cores, 1.5 GiB soft memory and 2 GiB maximum memory. Unrelated projects were left running. |
| Metrics | Missing counts could look like zero; mixed-age, late or private snapshots could distort publishing-slot comparisons. | Added collection backoff and sample counts. Slot comparisons use the same checkpoint and public captures within 30 minutes of the target, with median views. Missing values stay unknown. |

Channel and per-video counters refresh on different schedules and have different semantics. Their totals should not be forced to match. Lifetime views also mix very different video ages.

The largest videos include Wolverine/Night at the Museum (386,044 views), Red Hulk (190,465), Groot (70,745), Peacemaker (49,274) and Children of Men (45,539). Recent public examples also show demand: Stranger Things had 44,400 views, Scrubs 40,021, Pretty Woman 37,376 and Spider-Man 28,687 in the snapshot. These are observations, not proof that a topic or publishing slot caused the result. The Stranger Things count changed substantially during the audit, showing why an early view count is a weak verdict.

1. **Reduce reading load before changing the approved visual format.** Existing packages put approximately 32 words into 4.2 seconds, 35 into 6.2 seconds and 30 into 3.7 seconds—roughly 457, 339 and 486 words per minute. This is a plausible comprehension problem, not a measured retention finding. Test an 8–14-word hook expressing one verified surprise, with context in the description. Keep the exact scene, original audio and layout; vary one editorial factor per comparison.
2. **Make factual evidence the editorial gate.** Prefer a primary interview, production commentary or documented account over a competitor's caption. Separate what visibly happens in the scene from claims about why it happened. The new controls enforce a record and confirmation, while your judgment remains essential. The rejected Meet Bill claim should stay rejected unless credible new evidence changes the assessment.
3. **Choose scenes with an immediate, understandable payoff.** The strongest observed examples often involve recognizable characters and a visible surprise. Develop original, independently supported stories using that mechanism. Do not repeat a published story just because a different source URL or title appears to be new. Keep the configured 5M-view discovery threshold for now; evaluate whether it excludes useful original opportunities once you have comparable results.
4. **Use equal-age measurements.** Compare 24-hour and 48-hour public checkpoints, then collect engaged views, average percentage viewed, audience retention and viewed-versus-swiped-away from YouTube Analytics before changing slot times or blaming the renderer. Those Analytics measures were unavailable in this audit; Data API views alone cannot establish retention, CTR, revenue or conversion. Avoid declaring a trend from a few young uploads.
5. **Move to a named Cloudflare tunnel when a domain/account is available.** The current Quick Tunnel meets the requested deployment without an account password, but its hostname changes on restart and has no production SLA. The stable VPS entry redirects automatically to the current hostname. A named tunnel with a custom hostname is the next hosting improvement.
6. **Add encrypted off-VPS backups.** The installed daily snapshots protect against local application mistakes, but share the same VPS disk. Host loss requires an external copy plus the fixed brand/gameplay assets and source code. Keep credential backups private.

Password sign-in is intentionally disabled. Anyone who can reach the dashboard can access its contents and use its controls; Cloudflare proxying and CSRF protection do not restrict who may operate it. This is the current deployment setting you requested.
